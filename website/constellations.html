You will discover the constellations of our starry sky

There are 88 constellations in total.

The two most well-known patterns are the Big Dipper and the Little Dipper, which are part of the constellations Ursa Major and Ursa Minor.

A constellation is a group of stars that, seen from Earth, forms a recognizable figure in the sky. These figures are not real in the sense that the stars are not actually connected: they are often very far apart from one another. Observers have linked them together, sometimes to represent animals, heroes, or objects.

Astronomers have officially recognized 88 constellations. Some are very ancient, handed down from antiquity by the Greeks, the Babylonians, and other peoples. They were once used to navigate at sea, follow the seasons, and organize the agricultural calendar.

A constellation should not be confused with an asterism, which is a recognizable pattern but usually smaller. The Big Dipper, for example, is an asterism located within the constellation Ursa Major.
