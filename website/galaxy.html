You will discover the galaxies of our universe

The Galaxies Shown The image presents the three main types of galaxies, with an illustration on top and a real photographed galaxy below for each type.

Elliptical galaxy: ESO 325-G004. This galaxy looks like a large, smooth, oval ball of light with no arms or visible structure. It is yellow-orange in color with a very bright center, which indicates an older population of stars. It contains little gas and dust, so very few new stars are forming. In the photo, you can also see foreground stars from our own galaxy and distant galaxies in the background.

Spiral galaxy: M101, also known as the Pinwheel Galaxy. It has a bright golden core surrounded by large arms that sweep outward in a pinwheel pattern. The arms are bluish and dotted with pink and red patches and dark dust lanes. The blue color comes from young, hot stars, the pink patches mark regions where new stars are being born, and the dark lanes are clouds of dust. Along with irregular galaxies, spirals are among the most active types when it comes to star formation.

Irregular galaxy: NGC 1569. This galaxy has no defined shape and appears as a chaotic cloud of gas and dust. It glows in bright red and pink, with a lighter patch near the center. The red color comes from gas heated by young, massive stars, which points to intense star formation.

In summary, elliptical galaxies are smooth and oval, dominated by old stars, with very little star formation. Spiral galaxies have winding arms, a mix of old and young stars, and active star formation. Irregular galaxies have no clear shape, are dominated by young stars, and show intense star formation.
